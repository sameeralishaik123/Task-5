Titanic Dataset: Exploratory Data Analysis

Objective:To extract insights about the survival of passengers using visual and statistical analysis.

Dataset Overview:
- Total Records: 891
- Features include: Age, Sex, Pclass, Fare, Embarked, etc.
- Missing data handled in 'Age' and 'Embarked'.


Key Insights from the Data:

1. Gender (Sex):
- Females had a much higher survival rate than males.
- Survival was strongly influenced by gender, supporting the "women and children first" policy.

2. Passenger Class (Pclass):
- 1st class passengers had the highest chance of survival, followed by 2nd class.
- 3rd class passengers had the lowest survival rate.
- There is a clear relationship between class and survival, indicating socioeconomic advantage.

3. Age:
- Younger passengers were more likely to survive.
- Most passengers were aged between 20–40 years.
- Children had relatively better survival chances.

4. Fare:
- Passengers who paid higher fares had a better chance of survival.
- This suggests passengers in more expensive (higher-class) cabins were prioritized during evacuation.

5. Correlation Analysis:
- Survival is positively correlated with Fare and Sex.
- Survival is negatively correlated with Pclass.
- These are important features for building predictive models.

Additional Observations:
- Several outliers are present in Fare and Age columns.
- Most passengers boarded from Southampton, but survival rates were slightly higher for passengers from Cherbourg.

Conclusion:
Survival on the Titanic was influenced by:
- Gender (Females were more likely to survive)
- Passenger Class (1st class had the best survival rate)
- Age (Younger passengers had better survival odds)
- Fare (Higher fare correlated with higher survival)
